## SLIDE 2 — Problem Statement
The global e-commerce fashion industry is expanding at an unprecedented rate, yet it is critically hindered by staggering product return volumes. A primary driver of this return rate is the cognitive and visual gap consumers experience when attempting to visualize how a two-dimensional apparel catalogue image will map onto their specific, three-dimensional body type and pose. This uncertainty leads to bulk-ordering behavior where consumers over-purchase for size or fit testing, resulting in immense environmental and economic costs tied to reverse logistics. Currently, the industry lacks robust, user-centric tools that bridge the domain gap between static product imagery and real-world consumer appearances without requiring complex 3D rendering or physical scanning hardware.

---

## SLIDE 3 — Solution Overview & Workflow
Our system completely resolves this visualization barrier by providing an end-to-end, AI-powered virtual try-on workflow directly accessible via a web interface. The user initiates the process by uploading a personal photograph alongside a desired target garment image. The platform then seamlessly orchestrates these inputs through our deeply integrated artificial intelligence pipeline. This pipeline structurally parses the human body, mathematically deforms the garment to fit the user's specific posture, and synthesizes a high-fidelity dressed output. To elevate the realism, the system generates a custom photorealistic background scene around the user, upscales the entire composite into a high-resolution portrait, and concludes by delivering a personalized, critically constructive fashion critique generated by a multimodal vision-language model.

## SLIDE 4 — Technology Stack
- **UI Layer:** HTML constructs the structural framework of the user interface, Tailwind CSS powers the responsive and highly aesthetic styling, and Jinja2 dynamically binds backend variables to the frontend.
- **Application Layer:** Flask serves as the lightweight and robust WSGI web framework orchestrating the entire backend, Python is the core execution language binding the deep learning models to the web, and python-dotenv securely manages our critical external API keys.
- **Computer Vision & AI Models:** PyTorch provides the GPU-accelerated tensor computation backbone, OpenPose estimates critical structural skeletal keypoints, Self-Correction Human Parsing (SCHP) generates semantic body category maps, U2NET extracts precise cloth silhouettes, the Geometric Matching Module (GMM) predicts spatial deformations with Thin-Plate Spline interpolation, and the ALIAS Generator handles the final appearance synthesis.
- **External AI APIs:** Real-ESRGAN performs deep neural upscaling for artifact-free super-resolution, Google Gemini serves as a multimodal fashion stylist for constructive critique, and HuggingFace Stable Diffusion Inpainting generates dynamic, photorealistic background canvases.

## SLIDE 5 — Core Pipeline Architecture
Our deeply integrated GAN pipeline operates through a mathematically rigorous multi-stage execution flow. Initially, the U2NET architecture ingests the raw target cloth image to generate a high-precision binary cloth mask tensor. Simultaneously, the SCHP module evaluates the user's photo to map 20 semantic human parsing classes, intelligently masking out the original clothing to create an 'agnostic' identity tensor containing only the user's head, hands, and lower body. The OpenPose Body25 model then evaluates the input to extract 18 specific skeletal coordinates representing the user's physical posture. These integrated tensors are passed into the Spatial Transformer-backed Geometric Matching Module (GMM), which computes a spatial deformation vector field to warp the flat target garment strictly onto the predicted posture topography. Finally, the ALIAS Generator—utilizing highly efficient Adaptive Layer-Instance Normalization blocks—composites the agnostic identity matrix, the warped garment tensor, and the refined human parsing maps to render the photorealistic try-on output.

## SLIDE 6 — AI Features
**Super-Resolution:** To ensure the final rendered garment texture and weave are displayed at premium e-commerce quality, the system passes the generated image through Real-ESRGAN. Architecturally, this network utilizes Residual-in-Residual Dense Blocks (RRDB) to hallucinate high-frequency texture details, executing a rigorous 2x upscaling that entirely removes the soft blurring inherent to standard GAN generation limits.

**Gemini Fashion Stylist:** We integrated the Google Gemini multimodal vision-language model directly into the Try-On loop. By passing both the raw user input and the newly generated dressed output along with strict prompting parameters, the LLM analyzes the visual state visually and synthesizes a highly critical, constructive fashion critique, commenting specifically on color harmony, sizing fit, and actionable styling advice.

**Background Replacement:** Operating under a text-guided latent diffusion model paradigm, we employ Stable Diffusion inpainting orchestrated after the GAN try-on process to protect human-parsing alignments from scene noise. A precise binary mask isolates the dressed user while the AI generates a surrounding environment conditioned directly on the user's text prompt. We finalize this stage by utilizing native mathematical PIL alpha compositing with a combination of max-filtering and Gaussian blur, mathematically choking the white-fringe edges to ensure a perfectly seamless, glow-free background replacement.

## SLIDE 7 — Open Problems in Virtual Try-On Research
Despite tremendous architectural advancements, significant open problems continue to challenge the virtual try-on domain. First, handling lighting and shadow inconsistency remains difficult, as synthetically warped target garments rarely adopt the ambient illumination maps of complex generated background scenes. Second, GAN-based thin-plate spline warping algorithms mathematically struggle and tear when dealing with heavily occluded poses, crossed arms, or extreme non-frontal angles. Third, a deep domain gap exists between structurally clean, flat-lay product catalogue images and real-world noisy, uniquely-lit consumer selfies, causing feature extraction collisions. Fourth, accurately modeling complex fabric physics—such as rigid draping, localized folding, or material stretch tension—across diverse human body types requires rendering calculations beyond current 2D spatial transformer capabilities. Lastly, building these highly robust models relies inherently on supervised paired training, yet large-scale paired datasets containing identical humans in different poses or identical clothes on different bodies remain incredibly sparse in academia.

## SLIDE 9 — Future Scope
1) Replacing the current ALIAS GAN backbone with a natively trained Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) represents a vital next step, promising substantially higher visual fidelity, advanced material texture retention, and significantly better robustness to complex occluded poses.
2) Implementing real-time algorithmic inference optimization through Int8 model quantization and NVIDIA TensorRT compilation will be heavily pursued to achieve the sub-second latency required for seamless mobile application deployment.
3) Expanding the pipeline to support full outfit multi-clothing synthesis—including the simultaneous spatial fitting of complex multi-layered garments, trousers, and dynamic accessories—remains a highly critical commercial goal.
4) Leveraging single monocular images to deploy deep depth-estimation algorithms for predicting exact bodily measurements and volumetric body scaling, allowing the system to recommend precise garment sizing recommendations synchronously alongside the visual rendering.
